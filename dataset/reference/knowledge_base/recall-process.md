# Recall Process

## What Is a Safety Recall

A safety recall is issued by a vehicle manufacturer, or ordered by the National Highway
Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), when a vehicle or equipment creates an unreasonable
safety risk or fails to meet a federal safety standard. Manufacturers are required by law to
repair the defect at no cost to the owner.

## How Meridian Assist Checks Recalls

Meridian Assist looks up open recall campaigns using your vehicle's year, make, and model, or
its 17-character Vehicle Identification Number (VIN), against live data from NHTSA. This is
the same public database used by dealerships nationwide.

Because the public NHTSA recall lookup reports campaigns by year, make, and model rather than
the completion status of an individual vehicle, a recall showing as open for your model does
not necessarily mean the repair has not already been performed on your specific vehicle. Our
service team confirms your VIN's exact recall completion status when you check in.

## What Happens Next

1. Meridian Assist reports any open recall campaigns tied to your vehicle, including the
   campaign number, the affected component, and a short summary in plain language.
2. Recall repairs are always free of charge and are repaired using genuine parts.
3. You can book a recall appointment directly through the assistant or by calling your nearest
   location.
4. Some recall repairs require a specific part to be ordered before the appointment; the
   service advisor will confirm part availability when you book.

## Multiple Open Recalls

If your vehicle has more than one open recall, Meridian Assist will list up to three in chat
(one at a time in a phone or voice conversation) and can schedule a single visit that addresses
all open campaigns together whenever parts and technician time allow.

## Data Source and Attribution

Recall data is provided by the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and
is public domain government data.
